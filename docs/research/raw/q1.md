Based strictly on the sources in this notebook, evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems requires decomposing the pipeline into two interconnected stages: the **retrieval stage** (evaluating how well candidate evidence chunks are fetched and ranked) and the **generation stage** (evaluating factual grounding, answer quality, and attribution fidelity) [1-4].

---

### **1. Retrieval Metrics**

Retrieval metrics measure candidate coverage and ranking order before the context is fed into the generator [1, 5, 6].

#### **Recall@K (and Hit@K)**
* **Definition**: Measures the fraction of gold/relevant evidence documents or chunks that appear anywhere within the top-\\(K\\) retrieved results [5-9]:
  \\[\text{Recall@}K = \frac{|\text{Retrieved Relevant Docs in Top-}K|}{|\text{Total Relevant Docs}|}\\]
  *Hit@K* acts as a strict binary indicator of whether at least one fully relevant chunk appears in the top-\\(K\\) results [7, 10, 11].
* **When to Use**: Essential for evaluating **first-stage retrieval** (dense vector search, sparse BM25, or hybrid RRF fusion) [6, 12-14]. First-stage retrieval sets the absolute ceiling for the entire RAG pipeline; if the relevant passage is absent from the candidate pool (e.g., Recall@100 is low), no downstream reranker or LLM can recover it [6, 14-16].
* **Recommended Tools & Benchmarks**: Standard IR evaluation libraries, **BEIR** benchmark (evaluated via frameworks like RUMIR) [5, 17, 18], **MTEB** [19], **MIRACL** [19, 20], and **Future AGI / traceAI** [21, 22].

---

#### **Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR / MRR@K)**
* **Definition**: Evaluates how early the *first* relevant chunk appears in the ranked candidate list [6, 10, 23, 24]:
  \\[\text{MRR} = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q} \frac{1}{\text{rank}_q}\\]
  where \\(\text{rank}_q\\) is the position of the first relevant document returned for query \\(q\\) (assigning \\(1.0\\) for rank 1, \\(0.5\\) for rank 2, and \\(0\\) if no relevant document appears) [10, 23, 24].
* **When to Use**: Best suited for **factoid question answering** or single-document lookup scenarios where a single supporting chunk contains the complete answer and surfacing it at rank 1 minimizes latency and distraction [10, 23, 25, 26]. It serves as a strict ranking complement that ignores lower-ranked relevant documents once the first hit is found [6, 10, 27].
* **Recommended Tools & Benchmarks**: Standard IR tools, **BEIR** [2, 19], and **Future AGI** [21, 22].

---

#### **Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG@K / nDCG@10)**
* **Definition**: Evaluates the graded relevance of all retrieved documents across the top-\\(K\\) positions, applying a logarithmic position discount factor \\(\log_2(i+1)\\) to penalize placing relevant chunks at lower ranks [6, 9, 28-30]:
  \\[\text{DCG@}K = \sum_{i=1}^{K} \frac{2^{g_i} - 1}{\log_2(i + 1)} \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{K} \frac{g_i}{\log_2(i + 1)}, \quad \text{nDCG@}K = \frac{\text{DCG@}K}{\text{IDCG@}K}\\]
  where \\(g_i\\) represents graded relevance judgments (e.g., 0 = irrelevant, 1 = partially relevant, 2 = fully relevant) and \\(\text{IDCG}\\) represents the ideal DCG ordering [28-32].
* **When to Use**: The primary metric for **second-stage reranking** (cross-encoders like BGE-Reranker v2-m3, Cohere Rerank, and Qwen3-Reranker) and multi-document synthesis [6, 22, 28, 33, 34]. Rerankers typically do not introduce new candidates but optimize ordering; nDCG@10 measures whether the most informative chunks are concentrated at the very top of the LLM's prompt to avoid "lost in the middle" degradation [6, 33, 35, 36].
* **Recommended Tools & Benchmarks**: **BEIR** [5, 19], **BRIGHT** [22], **MTRAGEval (SemEval-2026 Task 8)** [3, 37], custom evaluation harnesses with graded human/LLM annotations [9, 31, 32], and **Future AGI** [22].

### **2. Generation Metrics**

Generation metrics evaluate the LLM generator's output given the retrieved context passages [3, 4, 13, 38].

#### **Faithfulness (Groundedness / Context Adherence)**
* **Definition**: The degree to which claims in the generated response can be logically inferred exclusively from the retrieved context without relying on parametric knowledge or introducing hallucinations [4, 13, 39-41]:
  \\[\text{Faithfulness} = \frac{|\text{Supported Claims}|}{|\text{Total Claims}|} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}(s_i \text{ is supported by } C)}{n}\\]
  where the response is segmented into atomic propositions \\(\{s_1, \dots, s_n\}\\) verified against context \\(C\\) [4, 39, 42, 43].
* **When to Use**: Critical for **safety, compliance, and hallucination prevention** [4, 13, 39, 44]. High retrieval metrics (Recall/nDCG) do not guarantee that the generator uses the context faithfully [13, 45]; measuring faithfulness isolates generator hallucinations from retriever omissions [4, 42, 46, 47].
* **Recommended Tools & Frameworks**: **RAGAS** [39, 41, 48, 49], **RAGChecker** [49-51], **Future AGI (`fi.evals.evaluate`)** [21], and **Splunk Agent Observability (Context Adherence)** [40, 46].

---

#### **Answer Relevancy (and Answer Correctness)**
* **Definition**: 
  * *Answer Relevancy*: Measures how directly the generated response addresses the user's initial question, regardless of factual correctness [41, 43, 52]. Typically calculated by prompting an LLM to reverse-generate questions from the output and computing embedding cosine similarity with the original query [43].
  * *Answer Correctness / Semantic Quality*: Evaluates semantic similarity between the generated answer and a gold reference answer (often using BERTScore F1 or human/LLM ratings) [51-53].
* **When to Use**: Used to ensure that the RAG assistant does not provide evasive, off-topic, or incomplete answers [41, 43, 52]. In production, a response might be completely faithful to an irrelevant chunk; Answer Relevancy flags whether the user's actual question was answered [43, 52].
* **Recommended Tools & Frameworks**: **RAGAS** [41, 43, 51], **ARES** [54-56], and **BERTScore** [53, 57].

---

#### **Citation Accuracy (Attribution Fidelity)**
* **Definition**: Evaluates whether explicit numeric citations or document references embedded in the response accurately point to the specific retrieved chunks that substantiate the corresponding statements [35, 41, 43, 58].
* **When to Use**: Crucial in high-stakes domains (legal, medical, technical docs) where claims must be auditable and traceable back to source documents [35, 41, 43, 59]. Studies note that general answer correctness does not automatically imply citation correctness [58].
* **Recommended Tools & Frameworks**: **RAGAS** [41, 43], **RAGChecker** [49, 50], and **Future AGI** [35, 60].

### **3. LLM-as-a-Judge Approaches & Frameworks**

Traditional surface metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) exhibit low correlation with human judgments on complex reasoning and NLG tasks [61-64]. The sources recommend LLM-as-a-judge frameworks calibrated against known judge biases [62, 65, 66]:

#### **G-Eval**
* **Mechanism**: A reference-free or reference-based evaluation framework employing **Auto-Chain-of-Thought (Auto-CoT)** and a **form-filling paradigm** [61, 67-70].
  1. Prompts the LLM with the task description and explicit criteria (e.g., Coherence, Consistency, Relevance) to automatically generate intermediate "Evaluation Steps" [67, 71, 72].
  2. Evaluates candidate text using the prompt and Auto-CoT in a form-filling template [67-69].
  3. **Probability-Weighted Scoring**: To eliminate integer score compression and tie-breaking artifacts on 1–5 Likert scales, G-Eval extracts normalized token logit probabilities \\(p(s_i)\\) and computes a continuous expected value [70, 73-76]:
     \\[\text{Score} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} p(s_i) \times s_i\\]
* **When to Use**: For fine-grained evaluation of text summarization, dialogue generation, and factual consistency where continuous distribution is needed without costly manual annotations [61, 73, 74, 76].
* **Weaknesses**: Exhibits self-preference bias toward LLM-generated texts and requires access to token log-probabilities [61, 77-79].
* **Tools**: Open-source Python package `nlpyang/geval` using GPT-4 or comparable foundation LLMs [80, 81].

---

#### **Prometheus 2**
* **Mechanism**: Open-source evaluator LLMs (**Prometheus-2-7B** and **Prometheus-2-8x7B**) built by merging weights from models trained separately on *Direct Assessment* (1–5 Likert scoring with reference answers and rubrics) and *Pairwise Ranking* (preference selection) using **DARE-Linear weight merging** [82-85].
* **When to Use**: When open-weight, transparent, cost-effective, and offline evaluators are required to assess custom user-defined rubrics without proprietary API costs or data privacy exposure [76, 82, 86, 87].
* **Tools**: Available on Hugging Face (`prometheus-eval/prometheus-7b-v2.0`, `prometheus-eval/prometheus-8x7b-v2.0`) and integrated into `distilabel` and `vLLM` [88-91].

---

#### **RAGAS & Claim-Based Frameworks**
* **Mechanism**: Automated decomposition of responses into atomic claims, comparing each claim against context chunks or reference sets [4, 39, 42, 47]. Generates synthetic evaluation test sets using knowledge-graph traversals and evolutionary query synthesizers (single-hop, multi-hop abstract, multi-hop specific) [92-96].
* **When to Use**: Automated end-to-end benchmarking of RAG pipelines, continuous CI/CD gating, and synthetic dataset creation without manual labeling [22, 95, 97, 98].
* **Tools**: `ragas` Python library, **ARES** (lightweight classification heads) [54-56], and **RAGChecker** [49-51].
